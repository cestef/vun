# 382. `lang.int.least(r)` and `lang.int.greatest(r)` give a bounded range's ends

Status: accepted
Where: 04, 11

`lang.int.least(r)` and `lang.int.greatest(r)` give a bounded range's ends (-128 and 127 for
`-128..128`), as `count` gives its size (spec 04). A property's integer of `Int(r)` is drawn as its
distance from the integer of the range nearest 0, bounded by the farther end, then, only where both
sides hold that distance, a side, above first (spec 11): a smaller choice is a smaller magnitude,
and failing distances stay contiguous as the side is kept, so shrinking a choice at a time finds the
least (`n: i8` failing below -100 reports -101, where reducing a choice modulo the range reported
-128; one choice alternating sides would stop at -102). The nearest integer is given a type holding
it alone (`at.to(Int(at..=at))`), so adding an offset from it spans `r` exactly, `i128` and `u128`
included
